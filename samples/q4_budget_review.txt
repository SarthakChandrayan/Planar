Meeting: Q4 Budget Review — Growth, Hiring & Infrastructure

Duration: 58 minutes

Participants:
Priya — CFO
Karan — Engineering Lead
Meera — Marketing Lead
Rahul — Sales Director
Ananya — People Operations
Dev — Finance Manager
Sameer — COO

Priya: Okay, are we all looking at the same spreadsheet? Mine says we're forecasting ₹1.8 crore in operating expenses for Q4, but Dev sent a version last night that says ₹1.94 crore. Before anyone panics, that might include the annual software renewal twice.

Dev: It does not include it twice. One version has the annual renewal under October expenses, and the other has it under prepaid expenses. But yes, the totals differ. I haven't reconciled the contractor invoices yet, so the ₹1.94 crore figure is provisional.

Sameer: Great. So we don't know whether we're over budget, and we're already having the budget meeting.

Priya: That's why we're here. The board approved ₹1.75 crore for Q4 operating expenses. We need to understand the gap, not pretend the forecast is final. Dev, can you reconcile those two versions by tomorrow afternoon?

Dev: Tomorrow afternoon is possible if procurement sends me the renewal schedule today. I'll message them after this call.

Karan: Before we cut anything, infrastructure spending has gone up because we're moving the analytics pipeline to the new cluster. We're currently spending around ₹8.5 lakh per month across cloud compute, storage, and managed databases. The forecast assumes ₹10 lakh per month by December.

Priya: Why the increase?

Karan: Traffic growth, more historical data, and the staging environment we added for the migration. The staging cluster is costing approximately ₹1.2 lakh a month. We could shut it down outside working hours, but I haven't checked whether that would interfere with overnight integration tests.

Sameer: Can we just shut it down at night?

Karan: Maybe. I'd want the platform team to check the test schedule first. If we shut it down blindly, we'll save money and then spend two days debugging failed deployments.

Priya: Karan, ask the platform team to estimate the savings from scheduled shutdowns and identify any testing dependencies. Don't implement anything yet.

Karan: Fine. I'll get an estimate by Tuesday.

Meera: While we're talking about spending, the proposed Diwali campaign budget is ₹18 lakh. That includes paid search, creator partnerships, landing-page work, and the agency fee. Last year's campaign was ₹14 lakh, but we had fewer channels and a smaller audience.

Rahul: Eighteen lakh seems high. How much revenue did last year's campaign actually generate?

Meera: Attributed revenue was ₹46 lakh, but attribution isn't perfect. Some customers would have converted without the campaign.

Priya: What's the target this time?

Meera: We'd like ₹60 lakh in attributed revenue.

Rahul: That's a target, not a forecast. Our sales pipeline doesn't support treating ₹60 lakh as guaranteed.

Meera: I didn't say guaranteed. I'm saying it's the campaign target.

Sameer: Could we cut the campaign to ₹15 lakh and keep the same target?

Meera: We could reduce creator spend, but that would mean dropping two of the larger creators. I can't promise the same outcome with less budget. We could instead run the first week with ₹10 lakh committed and release the remaining ₹8 lakh if early conversion metrics look good.

Priya: That sounds sensible, but what counts as good?

Meera: I haven't finalized the threshold. We'd need to agree on a minimum conversion rate and customer acquisition cost.

Dev: We should also account for refunds. Last time, the first-week revenue report included orders that were later refunded.

Meera: Correct. We should use net revenue after refunds, but I need to confirm how quickly the data becomes reliable.

Priya: Meera, prepare two options: the full ₹18 lakh plan and a ₹15 lakh alternative. Include the assumptions behind each, the proposed release conditions for the second tranche, and the refund-adjusted measurement method. Bring it to us on Monday. No one is approving either budget today.

Meera: Understood.

Ananya: On hiring, our original Q4 plan included six positions: two backend engineers, one data engineer, one recruiter, one customer success manager, and one sales development representative. At current compensation estimates, that's roughly ₹42 lakh in quarterly employment costs if everyone joins on schedule.

Priya: Does that include recruitment fees and equipment?

Ananya: Equipment, yes. Recruitment fees, no. The recruiter role is internal, so we assumed agency usage would decline. But the engineering roles may still need external sourcing.

Karan: We need the data engineer. The analytics migration is already taking time away from two backend engineers. If we don't hire, the migration could slip into next quarter.

Sameer: Do we need both backend engineers immediately?

Karan: Ideally, yes. But if we can only hire one, I'd prioritize the engineer with distributed systems experience.

Ananya: The recruiter is also intended to reduce external sourcing costs. If we postpone that position, we might pay more agencies in the short term.

Priya: What if we approve the data engineer and one backend engineer now, then defer the other four positions until the January review?

Ananya: That's workable from a recruiting perspective, but it doesn't automatically save the full projected amount. Start dates are uncertain, and offers already in progress could change the timing.

Rahul: I want to keep the sales development representative. We're expecting more inbound leads, and the sales team is already stretched.

Priya: How many leads are we expecting?

Rahul: Approximately 20% more than last quarter, based on the current marketing plan. That's a projection, not a committed volume.

Sameer: Could the existing sales team handle that increase for one quarter?

Rahul: Possibly, but response times may increase. I don't have a reliable capacity model yet.

Priya: Ananya, update the hiring forecast for two immediate engineering hires and show the cash impact of deferring the remaining roles. Rahul, provide the sales capacity assumptions and the impact of delaying the sales development representative. We will not approve the full six-role plan until we see both. Let's aim for Wednesday.

Ananya: I'll own the revised hiring model. Rahul, can you send your numbers by Tuesday?

Rahul: Yes, assuming Finance sends the latest pipeline report.

Dev: I'll send it tomorrow morning. It won't include the final campaign assumptions, though.

Rahul: That's fine. I'll label those assumptions as provisional.

Karan: One more thing on engineering costs. Our cloud vendor offered a 12% discount if we commit to an annual spend minimum. The commitment would be ₹90 lakh, paid monthly against usage, with a shortfall charge if we miss the minimum.

Priya: Is ₹90 lakh our expected annual spend with that vendor?

Karan: The current run rate annualizes to around ₹1.02 crore, but that assumes the December forecast is representative. If we optimize the staging cluster and reduce unused storage, actual spend could fall below ₹90 lakh.

Dev: And the shortfall charge could erase the discount. We need to model the minimum-spend risk rather than comparing the headline discount to list price.

Sameer: Could we negotiate a lower minimum?

Karan: We haven't asked yet. The vendor said the 12% offer expires on the 15th, but I haven't confirmed whether that's a hard deadline or a sales tactic.

Priya: Then we are not signing the commitment today. Karan, ask the vendor for a lower minimum and confirm the actual expiry date. Dev, model three cases: current run rate, optimized run rate, and a 15% usage decline. Include the shortfall charge and the net savings in each case. I want that analysis before we make a commitment.

Dev: I'll add it to the forecast work.

Sameer: Can we at least agree that if the discount saves money in the base case, we take it?

Priya: No. Not yet. The downside case matters, and we haven't reviewed the contract terms. This is an open decision.

Meera: Sorry, one related point. The agency contract renews next month. It's ₹2.4 lakh per month, and the agency has proposed a 10% increase for Q4 because we're adding the creator campaign.

Priya: Is that increase already in the ₹18 lakh campaign estimate?

Meera: Yes, but only if we keep the full campaign. The ₹15 lakh alternative assumes a smaller scope, so I need a revised agency quote for that option.

Dev: The current operating forecast includes the existing monthly fee but not the proposed increase. So depending on which campaign option we choose, the forecast needs updating.

Priya: Meera, get revised quotes for both campaign scopes and confirm which costs are already included in the budget model. Please do that by Monday so we don't count the agency fee twice.

Meera: I'll handle it.

Ananya: We also need to discuss the customer support contract. The vendor wants us to move from a monthly arrangement to a six-month commitment for a 9% discount. That would save around ₹1.1 lakh over six months if our ticket volume stays near the current level.

Rahul: Ticket volume is likely to increase after the campaign. We had a backlog last January.

Sameer: Can the vendor guarantee extra capacity if the campaign performs well?

Ananya: They said they can usually add agents within two weeks, but that isn't a contractual guarantee. We haven't negotiated service-level terms for the longer contract.

Priya: Then don't sign the six-month contract yet. Ananya, ask for a version with guaranteed surge capacity, cancellation terms, and explicit response-time commitments. Compare that with staying monthly. Bring the options to the next operations review.

Ananya: Okay. I'll contact the vendor this afternoon.

Karan: There's also a security renewal coming up. The monitoring vendor's annual contract is ₹6.8 lakh. We could switch to the cheaper plan at ₹5.2 lakh, but it has a lower event-retention limit.

Priya: How much retention do we actually use?

Karan: Usually around 70% of the current limit, although during incidents we sometimes exceed that. I haven't checked whether the cheaper plan retains enough data for our audit requirements.

Sameer: Can we cut the plan and buy extra retention only when needed?

Karan: Maybe, but I need to verify whether overage pricing is available and whether retention can be upgraded without migrating data.

Priya: Don't downgrade until Security confirms the retention requirements and the vendor clarifies the overage terms. Put that on the review list.

Dev: We're getting a lot of conditional savings here. We shouldn't add all of them to the forecast as if they're already realized.

Priya: Agreed. Let's separate committed spend, forecast spend, and potential savings. A potential saving doesn't reduce the approved budget until we've confirmed the assumptions and made the decision.

Rahul: What about travel? Sales requested ₹7 lakh for customer visits and the December industry conference. We spent ₹5.6 lakh last quarter.

Priya: Is the conference essential?

Rahul: It generates meetings with several enterprise prospects, but we don't have a reliable attribution number. We could send two people instead of four and keep the customer visits.

Sameer: I'd rather cut the conference entirely until the pipeline improves.

Rahul: That would be premature. Two of the larger deals we're working on came from last year's event.

Priya: We're not deciding that now. Rahul, split the ₹7 lakh into customer visits, conference attendance, and other travel. Give us a reduced option with two attendees and explain what customer coverage we would lose if we removed the conference entirely. Bring that with the sales capacity analysis on Tuesday.

Rahul: Okay.

Dev: One accounting issue before we close. The ₹3.6 lakh annual insurance renewal falls due in November. It's in the cash forecast, but only one quarter of it should be recognized as Q4 expense under our current accounting treatment.

Priya: Good. Make sure the budget comparison distinguishes cash paid from expense recognized. We keep mixing those numbers.

Dev: I'll add separate cash and P&L columns to the reconciled forecast. That should also help with the annual software renewal.

Priya: Good. That's part of tomorrow's reconciliation, not a separate project.

Sameer: We need a final answer on the budget gap. If the approved budget is ₹1.75 crore and the forecast is ₹1.94 crore, that's a ₹19 lakh gap. Can we say the gap is ₹19 lakh for now?

Dev: Only as a provisional comparison. The forecast includes unapproved campaign expansion, all six hires, and the unverified cloud commitment assumptions. It also doesn't yet reconcile contractor invoices.

Priya: Fine. We can state that the current provisional forecast is ₹1.94 crore against a ₹1.75 crore approved budget, a provisional difference of ₹19 lakh. We cannot call that the final overspend.

Karan: And the cloud discount shouldn't be counted as a saving until the minimum-spend analysis is complete.

Priya: Correct.

Meera: And the second campaign tranche isn't approved yet.

Priya: Correct.

Ananya: And the four deferred hires are still under review, not permanently cancelled.

Priya: Exactly. Deferral is not cancellation.

Sameer: Before we finish, what are we actually approving today?

Priya: We're keeping the existing approved Q4 budget of ₹1.75 crore as the control baseline. We're not approving the additional campaign spend, the annual cloud commitment, the six-month support contract, or the full hiring plan today. The two immediate engineering hires will be modeled as the proposed first hiring tranche, but we still need to review the revised forecast before final authorization.

Dev: I'll circulate the reconciled forecast tomorrow afternoon, assuming Procurement sends the renewal schedule today.

Priya: And please mark each item as committed, proposed, or unresolved. Don't quietly turn proposed savings into approved reductions.

Sameer: Fine. Let's review the revised numbers on Wednesday. If the gap is still there, we'll decide which proposals to approve, defer, or reject.

Meeting ends.
