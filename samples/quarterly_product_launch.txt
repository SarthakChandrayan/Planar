# Quarterly Product Launch Planning Meeting

**Meeting Type:** Product & Business Planning  
**Duration:** 52 minutes  
**Participants:**  
- Sarah — Product Manager  
- Rahul — Marketing Lead  
- Emily — Sales Lead  
- Karan — Customer Success  
- Meera — Operations Manager  
- Jason — Finance  
- Ananya — CEO  

## 1. Opening

Sarah opened the meeting by explaining that the primary objective was to finalize the launch plan for the company's new premium subscription package, currently scheduled for the first week of November.

The product has already been available to a small group of existing customers for approximately three weeks. Early feedback has generally been positive, particularly regarding the additional reporting features and priority support.

However, there has been some confusion around pricing.

The current proposal is to introduce the premium package at ₹2,499 per month, while the standard package remains at ₹1,499. Rahul mentioned that the marketing team had received mixed reactions during preliminary customer interviews. Some customers considered the difference reasonable, while others felt the premium package did not provide enough additional value.

Ananya asked whether the team had enough evidence to proceed with the proposed price.

Jason responded that reducing the price before launch would make it difficult to increase it later without creating customer dissatisfaction. He recommended maintaining the current price for the initial launch and evaluating conversion data after the first month.

The group generally agreed with this approach.

---

## 2. Customer Feedback

Karan presented feedback collected from approximately 40 existing customers who participated in the early-access program.

The strongest positive feedback was related to priority customer support and advanced reporting.

However, several customers said they would expect additional benefits if they were paying nearly ₹1,000 more per month.

One customer specifically suggested exclusive onboarding sessions.

Another requested additional user seats.

Emily said these requests could become problematic because sales representatives might start promising features that the product team has not officially committed to delivering.

Sarah agreed and said the company needs a clearly defined list of premium benefits before sales begins actively promoting the package.

The group discussed adding monthly consultation calls as a premium feature.

Karan supported the idea but warned that the customer-success team currently does not have enough capacity to provide personalized calls to every premium customer.

Meera suggested making the consultation optional and limiting it to one session per quarter.

After some discussion, the group agreed that quarterly consultation sessions could be included, provided the number of customers remains manageable.

---

## 3. Marketing Campaign

Rahul presented the initial marketing campaign.

The campaign would begin approximately ten days before launch and include email marketing, LinkedIn posts, customer testimonials, and a webinar.

The original proposal was to spend ₹8 lakh on the campaign.

Jason questioned whether that amount was justified given that the product had not yet demonstrated strong conversion rates.

Rahul argued that a smaller campaign would limit the company's ability to generate sufficient awareness and could make the launch appear unsuccessful even if the product itself performed well.

Ananya asked whether the campaign could be divided into stages.

Rahul proposed starting with ₹3 lakh during the first week, measuring engagement and registrations, and releasing the remaining budget only if predefined performance thresholds were achieved.

Jason agreed with this approach.

The team decided that the initial campaign would receive a ₹3 lakh budget, with additional spending requiring approval based on performance.

---

## 4. Sales Strategy

Emily explained that the sales team currently has approximately 120 leads that could potentially be converted to the premium package.

However, not all leads have demonstrated sufficient interest.

She proposed dividing the leads into three categories: high-intent, medium-intent, and low-intent.

High-intent leads would receive direct calls from sales representatives.

Medium-intent leads would receive personalized email sequences.

Low-intent leads would remain in the company's existing newsletter and automated communication workflow.

Sarah asked whether the sales team could handle the additional workload.

Emily said the team could manage it for the first month but might need temporary assistance if lead volume increases significantly.

Meera asked whether the company should hire temporary sales staff.

Ananya rejected that idea for now, saying the company should first determine whether the launch generates enough demand to justify additional headcount.

The team agreed to reassess staffing after the first four weeks.

---

## 5. Launch Date

The original launch date is November 4.

However, Sarah raised a concern that two important features are still undergoing testing.

The engineering team has indicated that both features should be ready by October 28, leaving approximately one week for final validation.

Karan argued that delaying the launch by one week would be preferable to releasing a product that creates a poor experience for customers.

Rahul disagreed, saying the marketing campaign has already been tentatively scheduled and delaying the launch would require changing several external commitments.

Ananya asked whether the two unfinished features were absolutely necessary for launch.

Sarah clarified that one feature is important but not essential. The second feature, however, is part of the main premium package positioning.

The group agreed that the launch should remain scheduled for November 4 for now, but October 28 would become the internal decision point.

If the critical feature is not stable by that date, the launch will move to November 11.

---

## 6. Pricing Discussion

Jason returned to the pricing question.

He presented three options:

**Option A:** Launch at ₹2,499 and keep the price unchanged for six months.

**Option B:** Launch at ₹2,499 but offer early customers a 20% introductory discount.

**Option C:** Launch at ₹1,999 and increase the price after establishing stronger adoption.

Emily preferred Option B because it would provide sales representatives with a stronger incentive when approaching existing customers.

Rahul preferred Option C because he believed the lower price would reduce friction during the launch campaign.

Jason strongly opposed Option C, arguing that lowering the price could create an expectation that the product is worth less than originally intended.

After discussion, Ananya proposed launching at ₹2,499 with a limited-time 15% discount for existing customers who upgrade within the first 30 days.

The team agreed.

The discount would not be publicly advertised and would only be available to existing customers.

---

## 7. Operational Concerns

Meera raised an operational issue that had not previously been discussed.

If the launch performs significantly better than expected, the customer-success team could receive a large increase in support requests.

Karan estimated that the team could comfortably support approximately 50 new premium accounts during the first month.

Beyond that number, response times could increase substantially.

The team discussed hiring temporary support staff but decided against making a commitment before launch.

Instead, Karan will create a contingency plan that identifies external support resources and internal employees who could temporarily assist customer success.

The company will also prepare a collection of frequently asked questions and onboarding materials to reduce repetitive support requests.

---

## 8. Success Metrics

The team agreed that the launch should not be evaluated solely on revenue.

The following metrics were discussed:

- Number of premium upgrades
- Conversion rate from qualified leads
- Trial-to-paid conversion
- Customer acquisition cost
- Churn during the first 30 days
- Support request volume
- Average response time
- Webinar registrations
- Marketing-qualified leads

Jason recommended establishing specific targets before launch.

Sarah agreed and said the team should have a dashboard available during the first week.

Ananya emphasized that customer retention would be more important than maximizing initial sales.

The group agreed that the first 30 days would be considered the primary evaluation period.

---

## 9. Final Decisions

The team reached the following decisions:

1. The premium package will launch at **₹2,499 per month**.
2. Existing customers will receive a **15% upgrade discount for 30 days**.
3. The initial marketing budget will be **₹3 lakh**.
4. Additional marketing spending will depend on campaign performance.
5. Quarterly consultation sessions will be included in the premium package.
6. The November 4 launch date remains the target.
7. October 28 will be the internal readiness checkpoint.
8. If the critical feature is not stable by October 28, the launch will move to November 11.
9. No additional sales or support hiring will occur before launch.
10. Launch performance will be evaluated primarily over the first 30 days.

---

## 10. Action Items

**Sarah:** Finalize the premium package feature list and coordinate the October 28 readiness review.

**Rahul:** Prepare the first-stage ₹3 lakh marketing campaign and define performance thresholds for additional spending.

**Emily:** Segment the 120 existing leads and create the corresponding outreach strategy.

**Karan:** Prepare the customer-success contingency plan and premium onboarding documentation.

**Meera:** Identify potential temporary support resources without committing to additional hiring.

**Jason:** Finalize the financial model and establish acceptable customer acquisition cost thresholds.

**Ananya:** Approve the final launch communication and review the October 28 readiness assessment.

The meeting concluded with agreement that the launch should proceed on November 4 unless the critical product feature fails the October 28 readiness review.

The remaining unresolved issue is whether the existing customer-success team can handle demand if the launch significantly exceeds expectations.
